I'm joining the committee of our beekeeping association and I've been told I must sign this apiary hosting agreement along with the landowner policy it refers to. Could you explain it to me clause by clause in plain language, say what each clause actually asks me to do or gives up, and then list the risks to me as a member and to the landowner, including anything that is unclear or one-sided?

MARSHWICK AND DISTRICT BEEKEEPERS' ASSOCIATION
APIARY HOSTING AGREEMENT AND SAFE PRACTICE POLICY
Version 4, adopted at the annual general meeting on 14 March 2026

Parties. This agreement is made between the Marshwick and District Beekeepers' Association, a voluntary association with its secretary's address at 6 Cobbler's Row, Marshwick ("the Association"), the person or organisation who owns or has control of the land described in the Schedule ("the Host"), and the member of the Association who keeps the colonies at the site ("the Keeper").

1. Purpose
1.1 The Host allows the Keeper to place and manage honey bee colonies on the land in the Schedule ("the Apiary") for as long as this agreement lasts.
1.2 The Association supports the arrangement by providing training, a disease inspection service and a swarm collection service, on the terms in this document.

2. Term
2.1 The agreement starts on the date the last party signs it and continues for 3 years. It renews for further periods of 1 year unless a party gives notice under clause 12.
2.2 The Keeper may keep no more than 6 full-size colonies at the Apiary at any one time without the Host's written agreement. Nucleus colonies kept for the purpose of raising queens or for swarm control do not count towards this limit for up to 8 weeks in the season.

3. Membership and training
3.1 The Keeper must be a member of the Association in good standing, which means that all annual subscriptions are paid by 31 March each year.
3.2 A Keeper who has been keeping bees for less than 2 years must complete the Association's beginners' course and be accompanied by an experienced mentor for the first 12 inspections of the season.
3.3 The Keeper must register the Apiary with the national bee health database within 30 days of placing the first colony, and must keep the registration up to date.

4. Siting and layout
4.1 Colonies must be placed at least 10 metres from any public footpath, bridleway, road or boundary shared with a neighbour, unless a hedge or fence at least 2 metres high stands between the colonies and the boundary. The flight path of the bees must be directed away from the boundary.
4.2 A source of clean water must be available within the Apiary at all times from 1 April to 30 September, so that bees do not seek water in neighbouring properties.
4.3 Signs at each access point must say that bees are kept at the site and give the Keeper's telephone number.
4.4 Hives must be on stands at least 30 centimetres above the ground, and strapped down in areas that are exposed to wind or floods.

5. Colony health and inspections
5.1 The Keeper must inspect each colony for signs of disease at least once every 10 days from April to August, and at least once a month at other times when the weather permits. A record of each inspection must be kept for 3 years.
5.2 If the Keeper suspects American foulbrood, European foulbrood or an exotic pest, the Keeper must close the hive, tell the Association's disease officer and the national bee inspector within 24 hours, and not move any bees, honey, comb or equipment from the Apiary until told the position.
5.3 The Association's disease officer may enter the Apiary at any reasonable time, with 48 hours' notice to the Host, to inspect any colony. The Host agrees to allow access.
5.4 Colonies found to have notifiable disease will be treated or destroyed as instructed by the national bee inspector. The Association does not pay compensation for colonies destroyed.

6. Swarms and temperament
6.1 The Keeper must take reasonable steps to prevent swarming, including inspection for queen cells every 7 days during May, June and July.
6.2 If a swarm leaves the Apiary and settles on land that does not belong to the Host, the Keeper must collect it within 24 hours of being told, if it is safe to do so.
6.3 If a colony shows persistent aggression, for example by stinging people or animals at more than 20 metres from the hive, the Keeper must re-queen the colony within 21 days, or move it. The Host may require the Keeper to do so by written notice.
6.4 The Association's swarm collection service is free of charge for members. It does not apply to wasps, hornets or bumblebees.

7. Insurance and liability
7.1 The Keeper must maintain public liability insurance of at least 5 million pounds, which is included in the Association membership fee if the Keeper has not more than 20 colonies.
7.2 The Keeper is responsible for any injury, damage or loss caused by the Keeper's bees or by the Keeper's activities at the Apiary, to the extent that it is caused by the Keeper's negligence or breach of this agreement.
7.3 The Host is not responsible for stings or other harm to the Keeper, to guests of the Keeper, or to people who enter the Apiary without the Host's permission, except where the Host's own act or neglect has caused the harm.
7.4 The Association is not a party to any claim between the Host and the Keeper, and accepts no liability for the conduct of any Keeper, but will, where it can, help to settle disputes between them.

8. Payment
8.1 The Keeper will give the Host 2 kilograms of honey each year, or a hive rent of 40 pounds, as the Host chooses, payable by 1 October.
8.2 The Host may not charge any other fee.

9. Honey and products
9.1 The Keeper owns the bees, the hives, the equipment and everything the bees produce.
9.2 The Keeper must comply with all rules on labelling and the sale of honey and must not sell honey with the name of the Host or the Association on it without permission.

10. Pesticides and other users of the land
10.1 The Host must not spray pesticides, herbicides or fungicides on flowering plants within 100 metres of the Apiary without telling the Keeper at least 5 days before, so that the Keeper can close the hives or move them.
10.2 If land next to the Host's land is being used for crops that are sprayed, the Host will pass on any information that the Host receives.

11. Access
The Keeper and one helper may visit the Apiary at any time of day or night, by the access route in the Schedule, in a vehicle if necessary. The Keeper must close all gates and avoid damage to crops, livestock and tracks.

12. Ending the agreement
12.1 Either the Host or the Keeper may end the agreement by 3 months' written notice, to take effect on 31 January or 30 September. The Association may end it at any time if the Keeper ceases to be a member or seriously breaches clause 5.
12.2 On ending, the Keeper must remove all colonies and equipment within 28 days, and leave the site clean and tidy.

13. Disputes
Any disagreement between the Host and Keeper will first be referred to the Association's welfare officer. If the matter cannot be settled within 28 days, either party may take other action.

Schedule. Land: the paddock known as Long Meadow, Hawthorn Farm, Marshwick, about 0.6 hectares, with access from the lane on the east side by the green gate.
