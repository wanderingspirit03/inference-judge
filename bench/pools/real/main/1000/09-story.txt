**The Last Reel**

The Marenholt Film Festival had an office, in the sense that a drawer is a room. It was one long space above a bakery, with a window that did not open and a radiator that did nothing else. From the first of September until the closing night in October, five people worked in it, and by the second week they had stopped apologising to one another for the smell of bread.

Odile Varga had been programme coordinator for six years. Her job was to know where every film was, on what format, in whose hands, and whether anyone had paid for the rights. She was good at it in the way some people are good at carrying trays: without looking, and with an expression that suggested the whole thing might still end badly.

On the Tuesday before opening night she arrived at seven and found the director already there, standing in front of the whiteboard with his coat on. Bram Lindqvist was a tall man who dressed as if he expected to be photographed and then forgot about it halfway through the day. Today his scarf was on inside out.

"Tell me the opening film is in the building," he said, without turning.

"It's in the building," Odile said. "It's in a box under my desk. The courier came yesterday at five and I signed for it myself."

"And it works."

"I haven't watched it. Nobody's watched it, Bram. It's a hard drive with a very long number on it."

He turned then, and she saw that he had not slept. Behind him the whiteboard was full of her handwriting: eleven days, forty-two features, nineteen shorts, a retrospective of a Norwegian animator who had specified, in writing, that he did not want to be introduced by anyone who had not seen all of his work. Next to the board hung a rota in four colours that Priya, the volunteer manager, had made and laminated. Odile found the lamination slightly insulting, as though the plan could not be trusted to survive on paper.

"The mayor is coming," Bram said. "The mayor, the minister's deputy, and apparently somebody's cousin who writes for a newspaper in the capital. They will all sit in the Grand Hall at seven tomorrow and watch a film that nobody has watched."

"It was selected by a jury."

"Juries lie," said Bram, with great tenderness. "That's why we have them."

Odile took her coat off and hung it on the back of the door, where it joined four other coats. The radiator ticked. Downstairs the baker was starting the first batch, and the smell came up through the floor like a rumour.

Her phone buzzed on the desk. It was a message from her sister Mira, the first in nine weeks. It said only: "I'm in town. Can we talk after the festival?" Odile turned the phone face down and said, to nobody, that she needed a projector.

"We have a projector," said Bram.

"We have three," Odile said. "And one of them is the right one."
