## 1. Summary

The quarter was profitable but weaker than planned. Revenue was 612,400 against a budget of 640,000, a shortfall of 4.3 percent. Operating profit was 118,900 against a budget of 192,000. Three things explain most of the gap: the cost of covering two staff departures with temporary accountants, a write-off on the Kessler Foods engagement, and the licence fees for the new filing software, billed for four months in the quarter (one month in advance), which started before the old system could be switched off.

The most important operational event was a set of late filings in the last week of September for the self-employed client group, caused by the transition to the new software. Seven returns were filed after the deadline. The penalties charged by the tax authority total 3,150 and the firm has decided to pay them directly rather than pass them on to clients. This is covered in section 6.

Client numbers are stable at 912 active clients, down from 918 at the end of June. Fee income per client rose by 1.9 percent. The pipeline for the autumn corporate work is good, with five new engagements signed worth an estimated 84,000 in fees, but staffing for these is not yet confirmed.

## 5. Software migration

The firm decided in March to replace the old filing system, which is no longer supported by its vendor after 31 December, with a cloud product that connects directly to the tax authority's portal. The contract was signed on 12 May for three years at 6,900 a month. The plan was to run both systems in parallel for the month of July and switch over on 1 August.

What actually happened:
- Data migration of the client master data was completed on 24 July, nine days late, because of duplicate client numbers in the old system (about 140 clients had two numbers).
- Staff training took place on 29 and 30 July for all accountants. Attendance was 19 of 21. The two absent staff members were not trained until 11 August.
- The switch was made on 1 August as planned, but the old system was kept running for reference until 30 September. That is why Q3 shows four months of the new licence fee (27,600, because August was billed in advance in July) together with the last three months of the old one (7,000 included in the total of 34,600).
- The direct link to the tax authority portal for self-employed returns was not certified until 9 September, three weeks later than expected. During that time, returns had to be uploaded by hand.
- Eight support tickets are still open with the vendor, of which two are marked as blocking: the import of payroll data for clients with more than one employer, and the reminder emails that are not sent to clients with a non-standard year end.

The partners should note that the old system will not be supported after the end of the year. All working data must be exported and stored before then, and a decision on how long to keep the archive has not been made. The legal retention period for tax records is ten years.

## 6. Late filings and compliance

Between 24 and 30 September, seven returns for self-employed clients were filed after the deadline of 30 September for those who had requested the extension. The returns were filed between one and four days late. The tax authority has charged penalties of 450 per return, which makes 3,150 in total.

Root cause, as established by Elke Varga and Priya Natarajan on 3 October:
1. The reminder emails to accountants about approaching deadlines had been set up in the old system and were not recreated in the new one.
2. The deadline list was not checked manually in the last two weeks because of the pressure of other work.
3. Four of the seven clients had sent their documents only in the last week. The accountants waited for missing information instead of filing a provisional return, which the rules allow.
4. Two of the seven clients had previously been handled by Marta Soliz, and the deadline was not in the handover list.

Actions already taken: a manual deadline list reviewed every Monday by Elke, a calendar for each accountant with deadlines for their clients, and a written rule that a provisional return must be filed three working days before the deadline if documents are missing. The firm's professional indemnity insurer has been informed as a precaution. No claim has been made by a client.

A second compliance point: an internal audit of 40 client files chosen at random found that 6 had no signed engagement letter on file, all of them clients who joined before 2022. The partners decided in July that the missing letters must be obtained by 31 December. By 30 September, 2 of the 6 had been obtained.
