I have just joined the committee of our rowing club and I have to vote on this new membership and boat-use policy next week. I am not a lawyer and some of it is hard to follow. Please explain it to me clause by clause in plain language, and then tell me what the risks are for ordinary members, for the club, and for me as a committee member.

MEMBERSHIP, BOAT USE AND SAFETY POLICY
Tidewater Rowing Club (the "Club")
Version 4.0, to take effect on 1 January 2027, subject to approval at the committee meeting of 14 October 2026

1. Purpose and status

1.1 This policy sets out the rules for membership, the use of boats and equipment, safety on the water and the handling of accidents and complaints. It replaces the Membership and Boat Use Policy, version 3.2.
1.2 By joining or renewing membership, each member agrees to follow this policy. The committee may change the policy by majority vote, and must give members at least 28 days' written notice of any change that affects fees, insurance or safety rules.
1.3 Where this policy conflicts with the Club's constitution, the constitution prevails.

2. Membership classes and fees

2.1 The Club has the following classes of membership: Full (age 18 or over, rowing access at all permitted times), Junior (under 18, rowing only under supervision), Student (full-time students with a valid card, same access as Full), Social (no rowing access, use of the clubhouse only) and Honorary (granted by the committee, no fee).
2.2 Annual fees are set by the committee and published on the Club's notice board and website by 1 November each year. The fees for 2027 are: Full 310, Student 170, Junior 140, Social 60.
2.3 Fees are due on 1 January. A member who has not paid by 31 January loses rowing access until payment is received. A member who has not paid by 31 March is treated as having resigned, and any boat storage rights end.
2.4 Fees are not refunded if a member leaves during the year, except where the member is unable to row for more than six months on the written advice of a doctor, in which case the committee may refund up to half the fee for the remaining months.
2.5 A new member must complete a swim test and an induction on the water before taking a boat out alone. The induction costs 25 and is not refunded.

3. Swimming and fitness declaration

3.1 Every member rowing at the Club must be able to swim 50 metres in light clothing and tread water for two minutes. The member confirms this in writing at joining and again every three years.
3.2 Each member must declare any medical condition that could affect safety on the water, including epilepsy, heart conditions and diabetes. The declaration is kept by the Club secretary and shared only with the safety officer and the coach on duty.
3.3 A member who knowingly gives false information on a declaration may have membership ended under clause 11.

4. Use of boats and equipment

4.1 Club boats may be used only by members with rowing access, and only after signing the boat book with name, boat, time out and expected time back.
4.2 Single sculls may be used only by members who have passed the single scull assessment. Quads and eights may be used by any Full or Student member under the direction of a qualified cox or captain.
4.3 A member who damages a boat or equipment through carelessness must report it to the boat officer within 24 hours. The Club may require the member to pay for repairs up to 250 for each incident. Damage caused by a deliberate act or by rowing in conditions that the safety officer had declared unsafe may be charged in full.
4.4 Private boats stored at the Club remain the property of the owner. The owner pays an annual storage fee of 120 and is responsible for insurance of the boat. The Club is not liable for loss or damage to private boats while on its premises, except where caused by the Club's negligence.
4.5 Boats must be washed and put back in the correct rack after use. Members who repeatedly fail to do so may be asked to complete extra duty shifts.

5. Safety on the water

5.1 The safety officer is the person named by the committee for that role. The safety officer may suspend rowing at any time because of weather, river level, debris or any other hazard, and members must follow that decision.
5.2 The river flag system is: green for normal conditions, amber for novices to stay off the water and all others to row only in pairs or larger boats, and red for no rowing. Flags are shown at the clubhouse landing.
5.3 Every boat on the water after sunset or before sunrise must carry a white light at the front and back. Rowing in fog is not permitted.
5.4 Junior members may row only when a qualified coach is present, and the coach must have a current first aid certificate and background check.
5.5 A coaching launch with a working radio or mobile phone and a first aid kit must accompany any outing of novices and any outing under amber flag.
5.6 Lifejackets are required for all coxswains and for all members rowing under amber flag. They are available in the boat store and must be checked monthly by the safety officer.

6. Accidents and near misses

6.1 Any injury, capsize, collision or near miss must be reported to the safety officer on the same day, using the form in the boat store or the online form.
6.2 The safety officer keeps a log and reports it to the committee at each meeting, without naming the member unless the member agrees or the committee needs the name to decide on a safety matter.
6.3 After any serious injury, the committee must review the incident within 14 days and decide whether to change procedures. Serious means any injury needing hospital treatment.
6.4 The Club will inform its insurer of all incidents that may lead to a claim. A member must not admit liability on the Club's behalf or agree to pay compensation without the committee's written agreement.

7. Insurance and liability

7.1 The Club holds public liability insurance of 5 million. This covers claims by third parties for injury or damage caused by the Club's activities. It does not cover injury to a member caused only by the member's own actions.
7.2 Each member takes part in rowing at their own risk, to the extent allowed by law. Nothing in this policy limits liability for death or personal injury caused by the Club's negligence, or for fraud.
7.3 Members are encouraged to hold personal accident insurance. The affiliated national body offers a policy to members for about 18 a year.
7.4 Guests may row once as a taster under the supervision of a qualified member, after signing a waiver. A guest may take part in no more than two tasters in a year.

8. Clubhouse, keys and duties

8.1 Each Full, Student and Social member must complete six hours of club duty each year, such as bar shifts, cleaning, boat maintenance or helping at regattas. Juniors are excused. Members who do not complete their hours by 30 November pay a levy of 15 for each missing hour.
8.2 Members holding keys to the clubhouse must not copy them or lend them. A lost key must be reported within 48 hours, and the replacement lock may be charged to the member up to 80.
8.3 Alcohol may be served in the clubhouse only by the bar volunteers, and must not be consumed while using boats or while on the landing stage.

9. Data protection

9.1 The Club keeps members' names, contact details, emergency contacts, medical declarations and payment records. These are used only to run the Club, to contact members and in an emergency.
9.2 Medical declarations are held by the secretary in a locked cabinet or encrypted file and are deleted two years after a member leaves.
9.3 Photographs may be taken at events and used in Club publications. A member who does not wish to appear must tell the secretary in writing, and the Club will make reasonable efforts to respect this.

10. Complaints

10.1 A member with a complaint should first speak to the person involved or the relevant officer. If this does not resolve it, the member may write to the secretary, who will pass it to the committee. The committee will reply in writing within 28 days.
10.2 A member who is not satisfied may ask for the complaint to be heard by a panel of three members who are not on the committee. The panel's decision is final.

11. Suspension and removal

11.1 The committee may suspend or end a membership if a member seriously breaches this policy, endangers others, damages the Club's reputation or fails to pay money owed.
11.2 Before taking such a step, the committee must tell the member in writing what is alleged and give at least 14 days to reply. The member may bring one other member for support to any meeting about the matter.
11.3 A member whose membership is ended loses all fees paid for the rest of the year, except where the committee decides otherwise.
11.4 In an emergency, the safety officer or the chair may suspend a member immediately for up to 7 days while the matter is considered under clause 11.2.

12. Racing, regattas and selection

12.1 The captain of the Club selects crews for regattas and head races after consulting the coach. Selection is based on recent performance in trials, attendance at training and availability on the race day. The captain must tell members who were not selected the reasons if they ask.
12.2 A member selected for a crew who withdraws less than 72 hours before the race, other than for illness or injury, may be asked to pay any entry fee already paid on their behalf, up to 40.
12.3 Entry fees for regattas are paid by the Club for Junior members and by the member in all other cases, unless the committee decides to support a particular event.
12.4 Members racing under the Club's colours must wear the Club's racing kit, which costs 55 and is bought once. The Club may lend kit to a member in financial difficulty, and any such request is kept confidential.
12.5 Trailers and vehicles used to carry Club boats to events must be driven by a member who holds the correct licence for the combined weight. The Club pays fuel and trailer costs, and members are asked to share lifts where possible.

13. Conduct, social media and welfare

13.1 Members must treat each other, coaches, visitors and other river users with respect. Bullying, harassment, discrimination and abusive language are not accepted, whether in person, on the water or online.
13.2 The committee appoints a welfare officer who is trained in safeguarding. Any concern about the welfare of a Junior member must be reported to the welfare officer at once. The welfare officer decides whether the matter must be reported to outside authorities, and the committee must not delay a report to take a vote.
13.3 Adults must not be alone with a Junior member in a changing room, a boat or a private vehicle, except where a parent or guardian has agreed in writing. Coaches should communicate with Junior members only through group messages that include a parent or guardian.
13.4 Members who post about the Club on social media must not share images of Junior members without the written agreement of a parent or guardian, and must not post material that could identify a member's home address or routine.
13.5 Complaints about conduct follow clause 10, except that complaints about the safeguarding of a Junior member go directly to the welfare officer and the chair.

14. Environment and river users

14.1 Members must give way to other river users in accordance with the national river rules, and must keep the bank clear of rubbish. Fuel, paint and cleaning products must be stored in the locked cupboard in the boat store.
14.2 Members must not row through the nesting area marked by the buoys between 1 March and 31 July, and must slow down when passing moored boats and anglers.
14.3 The Club takes part in an annual river clean-up day. Taking part counts for two hours of duty under clause 8.1.

15. Transitional provisions

15.1 A member who passed the single scull assessment under version 3.2 is treated as having passed it under this policy.
15.2 The fees in clause 2.2 apply from 1 January 2027. Members who paid their 2027 fee early at the 2026 rate will not be asked to pay the difference.
15.3 The six hour duty requirement in clause 8.1 applies in full from 1 January 2027. For 2026, the old requirement of four hours remains.

16. Changes and review

16.1 This policy is reviewed every two years. Any member may propose a change by giving the secretary a written proposal, signed by at least five members, at least 21 days before an annual general meeting.
16.2 The secretary will keep a copy of each version and make the current version available on request and on the website.

17. Definitions

17.1 "Committee" means the elected officers of the Club: the chair, the secretary, the treasurer, the captain, the safety officer, the boat officer, the welfare officer and two ordinary members.
17.2 "Qualified coach" means a coach who holds a current coaching award from the national body, a first aid certificate not older than three years and a satisfactory background check renewed every three years.
17.3 "Qualified cox" means a member who has passed the Club's steering assessment and has been signed off by the coach for the boat class concerned.
17.4 "Duty" means unpaid work for the Club agreed by the committee, recorded by the secretary on the duty sheet in the clubhouse.
17.5 "In writing" includes email to the address the member has given to the secretary, but does not include text messages or social media.
17.6 "Day" means a calendar day unless the clause says working day, and notice sent by post is treated as received two days after posting.
