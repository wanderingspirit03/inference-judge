Hello everyone,

Here are the figures again so nobody has to dig for them. In September we delivered 61,400 parcels. Harlow made up 9,800 of those, which is 16 percent of volume, but it produced 41 percent of all late deliveries. The average route in Harlow takes 3 hours 20 minutes for 38 parcels. The average route elsewhere takes 2 hours 45 minutes for 44 parcels. So a Harlow rider earns 155.80 for a shift, and a rider in the flat east side earns 180.40. Riders have noticed. Three of our best Harlow riders left in September, and two of them told Ellis that the pay was the reason.

My view is that we should move to a pay model with a base amount per parcel plus a distance and climb supplement. I have a draft that gives 3.60 per parcel, plus 0.35 per kilometre beyond the first 12 on a route, plus a fixed 6.00 for any route that crosses the Harlow ridge. On September volumes this would cost us about 7,300 more per month, but only if we change nothing else. I think it pays for itself if it keeps even four riders.

One caution: the supplement needs a way to measure kilometres that riders trust. Today the app records straight-line distance between stops, which understates hilly routes.

Priya

All,

I will be direct, as Marguerite asked. I do not think pay is the main cause of the lateness. I looked at the dispatch logs for the 31 worst Harlow days in September. On 22 of them the van that brings parcels from the depot to the Harlow hub left late, on average by 47 minutes, because the loading bay at the depot was blocked by returns from a large retail client. A rider cannot be on time if the parcels reach the hub at 08:50 and the promise window starts at 09:00 for the first drop.

So my position is: fix the depot loading order first, and keep pay as it is until we see the effect. If we change pay now we will not know what worked, and we will have a permanent cost increase for what may be a logistics problem.

I also want to say that the supplement idea is complicated. Riders will argue about every route. I would prefer a simple raise of the flat rate to 4.35 for Harlow only.

Tobias

Hi,

Some facts from the engineering side, because they affect both of your proposals.

1. The distance problem is real and I can fix it. We can switch the app from straight-line distance to road-network distance using the routing service we already license. It needs about two weeks of work and a test with ten riders. I could have it done by 23 October if I start Wednesday. 2. Tobias is right that the depot delay matters, but I think he overstates it. His 22 out of 31 days used the scheduled departure time. The real departure time comes from the van's own tracker, and I compared the two. On 14 of those 22 days the van left on time and was held up by traffic on the ring road between 08:15 and 08:45. So the depot explains about 8 days, not 22.

3. A Harlow-only flat rate would be unfair to the riders on the northern edge of the east side, whose routes are also long. A supplement based on real distance would not have that problem.

4. For Q4, the dispatch tool cannot handle more than 140 active riders at once without slowing down. We are at 112. So the hiring plan has an upper limit of 28 more riders unless I get a week to change the tool.
