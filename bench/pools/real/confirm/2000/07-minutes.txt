**Ashgrove Botanical Garden: Palm House Restoration Steering Meeting**
Date: Tuesday 6 October 2026, 14:00 to 15:50, Committee Room
Present: Marguerite Oyelaran (Director, chair), Tomasz Wieczorek (Head Gardener), Prisha Venkataraman (Collections Curator), Dennis Lindqvist (Estates Manager), Fiona Castellanos (Finance), Aliyu Bello (Learning and Visitors), Joanna Pike (Development, minutes)
Apologies: Hendrik Vos (Friends of the Garden)

**1. Matters arising**
The structural survey of the Palm House ironwork was received on 28 September. The surveyor found corrosion at 14 of the 62 glazing bars on the south side and at the base of two cast columns, but no sign of movement in the foundations. Dennis confirmed the contractor, Fairweather Conservation, can start scaffolding on 9 November if the order is placed by 23 October. The Heritage Trust grant of 180,000 pounds was confirmed in writing on 1 October. It is conditional on the work being finished and the building reopened by 30 June 2027, and on a progress report in February.

**2. Scope and budget**
Dennis presented the agreed scope: replace the corroded glazing bars, repaint the whole frame, replace 340 broken or cloudy panes with laminated glass, renew the ventilation motors on the roof vents, and install new under-floor heating pipes in the central bed. Fiona summarised the budget. The contractor's quote is 214,000 pounds. Plant relocation and care is estimated at 18,000 pounds. Interpretation panels and new signs are 9,500 pounds. A contingency of ten percent of the contractor's quote, 21,400 pounds, was agreed. The total is 262,900 pounds. Funding is 180,000 from the Heritage Trust, 40,000 from the garden's reserves and 30,000 to be raised by the Friends' appeal. That leaves a gap of 12,900 pounds, which Joanna will try to close with a local business sponsorship before the end of the year. Fiona warned that the reserves can be spent only after the auditors sign off the accounts in November.

**2A. Contractor programme**
Dennis read out the draft programme from Fairweather Conservation. Scaffolding and temporary roof covers: 9 November to 4 December. Removal of glass and stripping of paint on the south side: 7 December to 29 January. Ironwork repairs and replacement of the 14 glazing bars: 1 February to 12 March. Repainting and reglazing: 15 March to 30 April. Ventilation motors and under-floor heating: 3 May to 28 May, carried out in parallel with the final coat of paint where possible. Scaffolding down and clean-up: 31 May to 11 June. Handover and commissioning of the heating: 14 June to 18 June. Dennis noted that the contractor does not work on site during the last week of December. Tomasz asked that heating commissioning leave at least a week before the plants are returned so that the humidity can settle; it was agreed that the plants will return the week of 21 June and the soft opening for Friends will be on Friday 25 June.

**2B. Actions carried over from the last meeting**
Fiona to obtain two alternative quotes for the glass: done, both were higher than Fairweather's price. Aliyu to survey school teachers about winter visits: done, 34 replies received. Joanna to check whether the grant can be used for interpretation: confirmed, the signs are eligible but not the film.

**3. Plants**
Prisha reported that 71 plants in the Palm House must be moved. Of these, 23 are large specimens, including the two oldest cycads and the 9-metre Chilean wine palm, which cannot be moved whole. After discussion it was agreed that the wine palm will be left in place and protected with a timber and foam enclosure, with the heating off but a frost-protection heater inside. Tomasz said the cycads need to be moved before the nights fall below 5 degrees, so the move should happen in the week of 19 October. The other 48 smaller plants will go to the Temperate House and the propagation unit; Tomasz said these two spaces can take about 40 plants between them without crowding the stock plants, so about eight plants need another home. Prisha will ask the partner garden at Marlowe Park whether it can host eight plants until August. All plants have to be photographed, labelled and logged in the collections database before they leave.

**4. Visitors and learning**
Aliyu explained that closing the Palm House will remove the main wet-weather attraction and school groups booked for the winter will be affected. Eleven school visits are booked between November and March. He proposed to move six of these to the Temperate House with a new "plants in transit" workshop and to offer the other five a refund or a spring date. A viewing platform with a window on the scaffolding was suggested by the chair. Dennis said this would need a risk assessment from the contractor and a separate fence, and would cost about 6,000 pounds. The committee decided to ask the contractor for a price but not to commit. Aliyu will also prepare a short film of the restoration with the Friends' volunteers.

**5. Communications and fundraising**
Joanna will launch the Friends' appeal on 2 November with a letter to all 3,100 members. The target of 30,000 pounds is based on last year's appeal, which raised 22,500 pounds from 410 donors. A "name a pane" idea (a donation of 50 pounds in exchange for a name on a list in the reopened building) was welcomed. Fiona asked that the list only be published with donors' consent. The press release about the grant will be sent on 12 October, after the chair has spoken to the Heritage Trust's regional officer.

**6. Risks mentioned**
The chair asked each person to give their main worry. Dennis: more corrosion found once the paint is stripped, which could push the cost beyond the contingency. Tomasz: a cold snap before the cycads are moved. Prisha: loss of labels or records during the move. Fiona: the cash flow before the grant instalments arrive; the Trust pays 50 percent on signing and the rest on completion. Aliyu: school groups cancelling and not rebooking. Joanna: the appeal falling short.

**7. Decisions**
The committee approved the scope and the budget of 262,900 pounds. The order will be placed with Fairweather Conservation by 23 October after Fiona confirms the cash flow. The reopening date is set for Saturday 26 June 2027, four days before the grant deadline.

**8. Any other business and date of next meeting**
None. The next steering meeting is on Tuesday 3 November at 14:00.
